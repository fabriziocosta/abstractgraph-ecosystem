\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

There is unlikely to be one representation that is best for every graph
problem. The useful choice is the one that exposes the distinctions a question
requires without paying for many that it does not. Abstract Graphs make that
choice explicit: structural operators can be composed in a common space while
their outputs remain tied to the base graph. Controlled graph pairs tell us
what an expression can see; simple probes tell us whether that information is
accessible; and Pareto frontiers tell us what it costs. The experiments must
still provide those answers. Until their artifacts are frozen, the paper's
conclusion is a testable way to compare representations rather than a claim
that one expression wins.
