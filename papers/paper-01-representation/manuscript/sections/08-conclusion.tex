\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

There is unlikely to be one representation that is best for every graph
problem. The useful choice is the one that exposes the distinctions a question
requires without paying for many that it does not. Abstract Graphs make that
choice explicit: structural operators can be composed in a common space while
their outputs remain tied to the base graph. The decisive tests compare that
composition with faithful baseline translations, atomic expressions, and
lossless feature concatenation. Mapped witnesses ask whether a distinction can
be localized, while factor-specific Pareto frontiers ask what it costs. The
experiments must still provide those answers. Until their artifacts are frozen,
the contribution is a falsifiable comparison method, not evidence that
composition is inherently superior.
