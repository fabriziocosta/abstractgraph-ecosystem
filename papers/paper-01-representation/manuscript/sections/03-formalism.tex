\section{Abstract Graph Formalism}
\label{sec:formalism}

Let
\begin{equation}
  \basegraph=(V,E,X_V,X_E)
\end{equation}
be an attributed base graph and let \(\interpgraph=(V_I,E_I)\) be an
interpretation graph. A mapping
\begin{equation}
  \mapping:V_I\rightarrow\subgraphs(\basegraph)
\end{equation}
associates every interpretation node with a permitted subgraph of
\(\basegraph\). The working Abstract Graph representation is the triple
\begin{equation}
  \abstractgraph=(\basegraph,\interpgraph,\mapping).
\end{equation}

The complete formalism will specify the base-graph and attribute model,
interpretation-node and interpretation-edge semantics, mapped-subgraph
membership and overlap, structural identities and equivalence, and provenance.
In particular, \(\subgraphs(\basegraph)\) denotes the permitted mapped-subgraph
space; its induced-subgraph, connectivity, overlap, and attribute-preservation
rules remain to be fixed in the formal specification.
