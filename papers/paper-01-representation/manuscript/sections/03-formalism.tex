\section{Abstract Graph Formalism}
\label{sec:formalism}

Let
\begin{equation}
  \basegraph=(V,E,X_V,X_E)
\end{equation}
be an attributed base graph and let \(\interpgraph=(V_I,E_I)\) be an
interpretation graph. A mapping
\begin{equation}
  \mapping:V_I\rightarrow\subgraphs(\basegraph)
\end{equation}
associates every interpretation node with a permitted subgraph of
\(\basegraph\). The working Abstract Graph representation is the triple
\begin{equation}
  \abstractgraph=(\basegraph,\interpgraph,\mapping).
\end{equation}

The complete notation, equivalence relations, provenance definitions, operator
contracts, and representation invariants are collected in
\cref{app:formal-notation}. In particular, \(\subgraphs(\basegraph)\) denotes
the permitted mapped-subgraph space rather than the unrestricted set of all
NetworkX graph objects.
