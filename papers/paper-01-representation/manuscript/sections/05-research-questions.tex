\section{Validation and Research Questions}
\label{sec:research-questions}

Before comparing operator expressions, we must establish that the
representation behaves as intended. These checks validate the experimental
instrument; they are not the paper's scientific questions.

\paragraph{V1: Structural traceability.}
After composition and serialization, every interpretation object must remain
traceable to the base-graph nodes and edges from which it was derived.

\paragraph{V2: Invariance.}
Permitted node relabellings and attribute-order changes must produce equivalent
Abstract Graphs and identical deterministic feature signatures.

Once these requirements hold, the study addresses two questions.

\paragraph{RQ1: Structural discrimination.}
How does the choice and composition of Abstract Graph operators change which
structural distinctions are preserved, collapsed, and accessible after
vectorization? Intrinsic discrimination provides the first answer. Diagnostic
probes then test accessibility, while pooling and hash-width ablations locate
information lost after the operator expression has been evaluated.

\paragraph{RQ2: Discrimination--complexity trade-off.}
What computational cost is required for the distinctions exposed by different
operator expressions, and which configurations lie on the resulting runtime,
memory, and representation-size Pareto frontiers?
